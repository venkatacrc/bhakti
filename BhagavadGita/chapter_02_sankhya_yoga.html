Bhagavad Gītā — Chapter 2

अध्यायः २ · साङ्ख्ययोगः — The Yoga of Knowledge

Complete in Sanskrit, Telugu, English with word-by-word and full meaning. 72 verses.

Seeing Arjuna overwhelmed with grief, Krishna begins His teaching. He explains the eternity of the Self, the duty of a warrior, and the yoga of even-minded action without attachment to results — culminating in the portrait of the person of steady wisdom (sthita-prajña).

The Seventy-Two Verses

Verse 1
सञ्जय उवाच · sañjaya uvāca
तं तथा कृपयाऽविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम् ।
विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः ॥ १॥
తం తథా కృపయాఽవిష్టమశ్రు-పూర్ణాకులేక్షణమ్ ।
విషీదంతమిదం వాక్యమువాచ మధుసూదనః ॥ 1 ॥
taṁ tathā kṛpayāviṣṭamaśru pūrṇākulekṣaṇam |
viṣīdantamidaṁ vākyam uvāca madhusūdanaḥ || 1 ||
Words: tam (to him (Arjun)), tathā (thus), kṛpayā (with pity), āviṣṭam (overwhelmed), aśru-pūrṇa (full of tears), ākula (distressed), īkṣaṇam (eyes), viṣīdantam (grief-stricken), idam (these), vākyam (words), uvāca (said), madhusūdanaḥ (Shree Krishn, slayer of the Madhu demon)
Meaning: To him who had been filled with pity, whose eyes were filled with tears and showed distress, and who was sorrowing, Madhusudana uttered these words.
Verse 2
श्रीभगवानुवाच · śrī-bhagavān uvāca
कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।
अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥ २॥
కుతస్త్వా కశ్మలమిదం విషమే సముపస్థితమ్ ।
అనార్య-జుష్టమస్వర్గ్యమకీర్తి-కరమర్జున ॥ 2 ॥
kutastvā kaśmalamidaṁ viṣame samupasthitam |
anārya-juṣṭamasvargyam akīrti-karam arjuna || 2 ||
Words: kutaḥ (wherefrom), tvā (to you), kaśmalam (delusion), idam (this), viṣame (in this hour of peril), samupasthitam (overcome), anārya (crude person), juṣṭam (practiced), asvargyam (which does not lead to the higher abodes), akīrti-karam (leading to disgrace), arjuna (Arjun)
Meaning: O Arjuna, in this perilous place, whence has this impurity come to you, entertained by unenlightened persons, which does not lead to heaven and brings infamy?"
Verse 3
क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते ।
क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप ॥ ३॥

క్షుద్రం హృదయ-దౌర్బల్యం త్యక్త్వోత్తిష్ఠ పరంతప ॥ 3 ॥
klaibyaṁ mā sma gamaḥ pārtha naitat tvayyupapadyate |

Words: klaibyam (unmanliness), mā sma (do not), gamaḥ (yield to), pārtha (Arjun, the son of Pritha), na (not), etat (this), tvayi (to you), upapadyate (befitting), kṣudram (petty), hṛdaya (heart), daurbalyam (weakness), tyaktvā (giving up), uttiṣṭha (arise), param-tapa (conqueror of enemies)
Meaning: O Partha, do not yield to unmanliness. This does not befit you. O scorcher of foes, arise, giving up the petty weakness of heart.
Verse 4
अर्जुन उवाच · arjuna uvāca
कथं भीष्ममहं संख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन ।
इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥ ४॥

ఇషుభిః ప్రతియోత్స్యామి పూజార్హావరి-సూదన ॥ 4 ॥

Words: katham (how), bhīṣmam (Bheeshma), aham (I), sankhye (in battle), droṇam (Dronacharya), cha (and), madhu-sūdana (Shree Krishn, slayer of the Madhu demon), iṣubhiḥ (with arrows), pratiyotsyāmi (shall I shoot), pūjā-arhau (worthy of worship), ari-sūdana (destroyer of enemies)
Meaning: O Madhusudana, O destroyer of enemies, how can I fight with arrows in battle against Bhisma and Drona, who are worthy of adoration?"

हत्वार्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव भुञ्जीय भोगान् रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥ ५॥

Words: gurūn (teachers), ahatvā (not killing), hi (certainly), mahā-anubhāvān (noble elders), śreyaḥ (better), bhoktum (to enjoy life), bhaikṣyam (by begging), api (even), iha loke (in this world), hatvā (killing), artha (gain), kāmān (desiring), tu (but), gurūn (noble elders), iha (in this world), eva (certainly), bhuñjīya (enjoy), bhogān (pleasures), rudhira (blood), pradigdhān (tainted with)
Meaning: Rather than killing the noble-minded elders, it is better in this world to live even on alms. But by killing them, we shall only be enjoying here the pleasures of wealth and desirable things drenched in blood.
Verse 6
न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।
यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषाम स्तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥ ६॥

na chaitadvidmaḥ kataranno garīyo |
yadvā jayema yadi vā no jayeyuḥ yāneva hatvā na jijīviṣāmas te ’vasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ || 6 ||
Words: na (not), cha (and), etat (this), vidmaḥ (we know), katarat (which), naḥ (for us), garīyaḥ (is preferable), yat vā (whether), jayema (we may conquer), yadi (if), vā (or), naḥ (us), jayeyuḥ (they may conquer), yān (whom), eva (certainly), hatvā (after killing), na (not), jijīviṣāmaḥ (we desire to live), te (they), avasthitāḥ (are standing), pramukhe (before us), dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ (the sons of Dhritarashtra)
Meaning: We do not know which is better for us, whether we shall win or they shall conquer us. Those very sons of Dhrtarastra, whom we do not wish to kill, stand in confrontation.

Meaning: With my nature overpowered by weak commiseration, my mind bewildered about duty, I supplicate You. Tell me for certain what is better; I am Your disciple, having taken refuge in You. Instruct me.
Verse 8
न हि प्रपश्यामि ममापनुद्या द्यच्छोकमुच्छोषणमिन्द्रियाणाम् ।

Verse 9
सञ्जय उवाच · sañjaya uvāca

न योत्स्य इति गोविन्दमुक्त्वा तूष्णीं बभूव ह ॥ ९॥

Words: evam (thus), uktvā (having spoken), hṛṣīkeśam (to Shree Krishna, the master of the mind and senses), guḍākeśaḥ (Arjun, the conquerer of sleep), parantapaḥ (Arjun, the chastiser of the enemies), na yotsye (I shall not fight), iti (thus), govindam (Krishna, the giver of pleasure to the senses), uktvā (having addressed), tūṣṇīm (silent), babhūva (became ha)
Meaning: Having spoken thus to Hrsikesa (Krsna), Gudakesa (Arjuna), the afflictor of foes, verily became silent, telling Govinda, 'I shall not fight.'

Verse 11

Meaning: You grieve for those who are not to be grieved for; and you speak words of wisdom! The learned do not grieve for the departed nor for those who have not departed.

Words: na (not), asti (is), buddhiḥ (intellect), ayuktasya (not united), na (not), cha (and), ayuktasya (not united), bhāvanā (contemplation), na (nor), cha (and), abhāvayataḥ (for those not united), śāntiḥ (peace), aśāntasya (of the unpeaceful), kutaḥ (where), sukham (happiness)
Meaning: For the unsteady there is no wisdom, and there is no meditation for the unsteady person. And for an unmeditative person there is no peace. How can there be happiness for one without peace?
Verse 67
इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते ।
तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥ ६७॥
ఇంద్రియాణాం హి చరతాం యన్-మనోఽనువిధీయతే ।
తదస్య హరతి ప్రజ్ఞాం వాయుర్-నావమివాంభసి ॥ 67 ॥
indriyāṇāṁ hi charatāṁ yan mano ’nuvidhīyate |
tadasya harati prajñāṁ vāyur nāvam ivāmbhasi || 67 ||
Words: indriyāṇām (of the senses), hi (indeed), charatām (roaming), yat (which), manaḥ (the mind), anuvidhīyate (becomes constantly engaged), tat (that), asya (of that), harati (carries away), prajñām (intellect), vāyuḥ (wind), nāvam (boat), iva (as), ambhasi (on the water)
Meaning: For, the mind which follows in the wake of the wandering senses carries away his wisdom like a boat on the waters.
Verse 68
तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः ।
इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ६८॥
తస్మాద్-యస్య మహా-బాహో నిగృహీతాని సర్వశః ।
ఇంద్రియాణీంద్రియార్థేభ్యస్-తస్య ప్రజ్ఞా ప్రతిష్ఠితా ॥ 68 ॥
tasmād yasya mahā-bāho nigṛhītāni sarvaśaḥ |
indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyas tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā || 68 ||
Words: tasmāt (therefore), yasya (whose), mahā-bāho (mighty-armed one), nigṛhītāni (restrained), sarvaśaḥ (completely), indriyāṇi (senses), indriya-arthebhyaḥ (from sense objects), tasya (of that person), prajñā (transcendental knowledge), pratiṣṭhitā (remains fixed)
Meaning: Therefore, O mighty-armed one, this wisdom becomes established when all its senses are withdrawn from their objects.
Verse 69
या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी ।
यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥ ६९॥
యా నిశా సర్వ-భూతానాం తస్యాం జాగర్తి సంయమీ ।
యస్యాం జాగ్రతి భూతాని సా నిశా పశ్యతో మునేః ॥ 69 ॥
yā niśā sarva-bhūtānāṁ tasyāṁ jāgarti sanyamī |
yasyāṁ jāgrati bhūtāni sā niśā paśyato muneḥ || 69 ||
Words: yā (which), niśā (night), sarva-bhūtānām (of all living beings), tasyām (in that), jāgarti (is awake), sanyamī (self-controlled), yasyām (in which), jāgrati (are awake), bhūtāni (creatures), sā (that), niśā (night), paśyataḥ (see), muneḥ (sage)
Meaning: The self-restrained man stays awake during what is night for all creatures. That which creatures stay awake for, it is night to the discerning sage.
Verse 70
आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् ।
तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥ ७०॥
ఆపూర్యమాణమచల-ప్రతిష్ఠం సముద్రమాపః ప్రవిశంతి యద్వత్ ।
తద్వత్-కామా యం ప్రవిశంతి సర్వే స శాంతిమాప్నోతి న కామ-కామీ ॥ 70 ॥
āpūryamāṇam achala-pratiṣṭhaṁ |
samudram āpaḥ praviśanti yadvat tadvat kāmā yaṁ praviśanti sarve sa śāntim āpnoti na kāma-kāmī || 70 ||
Words: āpūryamāṇam (filled from all sides), achala-pratiṣṭham (undisturbed), samudram (ocean), āpaḥ (waters), praviśanti (enter), yadvat (as), tadvat (likewise), kāmāḥ (desires), yam (whom), praviśanti (enter), sarve (all), saḥ (that person), śāntim (peace), āpnoti (attains), na (not), kāma-kāmī (one who strives to satisfy desires)
Meaning: That man attains peace into whom all desires enter, just as waters flow into the sea that remains unchanged even when filled from all sides. Not so one who is desirous of objects.
Verse 71
विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः ।
निर्ममो निरहंकारः स शांतिमधिगच्छति ॥ ७१॥
విహాయ కామాన్యః సర్వాన్పుమాంశ్చరతి నిఃస్పృహః ।
నిర్మమో నిరహంకారః స శాంతిమధిగచ్ఛతి ॥ 71 ॥
vihāya kāmān yaḥ sarvān pumānś charati niḥspṛhaḥ |
nirmamo nirahankāraḥ sa śāntim adhigachchati || 71 ||
Words: vihāya (giving up), kāmān (material desires), yaḥ (who), sarvān (all), pumān (a person), charati (lives), niḥspṛhaḥ (free from hankering), nirmamaḥ (without a sense of proprietorship), nirahankāraḥ (without egoism), saḥ (that person), śāntim (perfect peace), adhigachchhati (attains)
Meaning: That man attains peace who, after rejecting all desires, moves about free from hankering, without the idea of 'me' and 'mine', and devoid of pride.
Verse 72
एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति ।
स्थित्वाऽस्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥ ७२॥
ఏషా బ్రాహ్మీ స్థితిః పార్థ నైనాం ప్రాప్య విముహ్యతి ।
స్థిత్వాఽస్యామంత-కాలేఽపి బ్రహ్మ-నిర్వాణమృచ్ఛతి ॥ 72 ॥
eṣā brāhmī sthitiḥ pārtha naināṁ prāpya vimuhyati |
sthitvāsyām anta-kāle ’pi brahma-nirvāṇam ṛcchati || 72 ||
Words: eṣā (such), brāhmī sthitiḥ (state of God-realization), pārtha (Arjun, the son of Pritha), na (never), enām (this), prāpya (having attained), vimuhyati (is deluded), sthitvā (being established), asyām (in this), anta-kāle (at the hour of death), api (even), brahma-nirvāṇam (liberation from Maya), ṛchchhati (attains)
Meaning: O Partha, this is the state of being established in Brahman. One does not become deluded after attaining this; one attains identification with Brahman by being established in this state even in the closing years of one's life.

॥ इति श्रीमद्भगवद्गीतासूपनिषत्सु ब्रह्मविद्यायां योगशास्त्रे श्रीकृष्णार्जुनसंवादे साङ्ख्ययोगो नाम द्वितीयोऽध्यायः ॥
Thus ends the second chapter of the Bhagavad Gītā, entitled Sāṅkhya Yoga — the Yoga of Knowledge.
